Afghanistan — Beyond the Headlines, Into the Heart of It

Afghanistan · Central Asia

Nobody goes to Afghanistan for leisure. I went because I had to know what was real. What I found was a country of staggering, heartbreaking beauty — ancient hospitality in the middle of modern chaos.

I stayed with Taliban hosts in remote areas, shared meals with people the world had largely written off, and stood at the feet of the Bamiyan Buddhas — the great carved giants the world mourned when they were blown apart, now silent cliff faces watching over a valley that still carries their memory.

The Silk Route — Following the Oldest Road in the World

Central Asia · Iran · Caucasus

Merchants, conquerors, monks, and wanderers have walked this corridor for two thousand years. I followed the route overland — through mountain passes, across steppe, through bazaars that smell of spice and dust and time.

The Silk Route is not a single road. It is a dozen overlapping trails, a web of empire and trade connecting East to West. Traveling it solo means making choices constantly: which path, which border, which stranger to trust.

2023 Solo · Accidental

Uzbekistan — The Remote Village I Found by Mistake

Uzbekistan · Central Asia

I did not plan to end up here. A missed connection, a wrong bus, a language barrier that turned into an invitation — and I was in a village so small it might not appear on any map I could find.

The people fed me without asking anything. The nights were cold and totally quiet. I took the longest train journey of my life to eventually leave, watching the steppe unspool for days through a smudged window, thinking about how the best things happen when you're trying to do something else.

2023 Solo · Camping

Kazakhstan — Almaty, Bekatata & the Mangystau Wilderness

Kazakhstan · Central Asia

Most people who go to Kazakhstan stop in Almaty — a cosmopolitan city backed against the Tian Shan mountains, where Soviet concrete gives way to alpine trails. I used it as a staging point for something further and stranger.

Western Kazakhstan — the Mangystau region — is one of the most alien landscapes on Earth. Chalk canyons, underground mosques carved into rock, the dead calm of the Caspian coast. I camped alone in Mangystau for days, cooking over a small flame, watching the light go strange in the evenings.

Bekatata, the underground mosque built by a 14th-century Sufi mystic into a cliff face — it is the kind of place that makes you understand why people have always needed holy ground.
