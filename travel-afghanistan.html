Nobody goes to Afghanistan for leisure. I went because I had to know what was real — beyond the news cycles, beyond the geopolitical shorthand, beyond the version of the country that exists in other people's imaginations. What I found was staggering.

Afghanistan is one of the most beautiful countries I have ever set foot in. That is not a sentence I expected to write before I went. The mountains, the light, the valleys — they carry a kind of severity that I have not encountered anywhere else on Earth.

Bamiyan — The Cliffs That Remember

The Buddha statues of Bamiyan are gone — blown apart in 2001 in one of the most deliberate acts of cultural destruction of the modern era. What remains are two enormous cliff-face niches, carved into the sandstone above a green valley. Standing in front of them, you feel the absence more than you see it.
